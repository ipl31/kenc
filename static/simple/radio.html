It started with a shortwave radio

A long wire antenna, late nights, and the world on the other end of a signal.

Radio

The first radio I remember having is a Realistic DX-440 shortwave receiver, a rebranded Sangean. I made a long wire antenna out of multiple pieces of speaker wire and spent countless nights crawling the bands looking for interesting broadcasts.

Before long, I stumbled into the world of Citizens Band (CB) radio and picked up a Realistic TRC-424, a 40-channel AM base station. As a teenager, I spent endless hours chatting with local CB operators, day and night.

I started experimenting with dipole antennas and soon began making long-distance contacts. An 18-foot mast-mounted omnidirectional antenna followed, and then came the radio upgrade: a Ranger RCI-2950.

I got skilled at working the 26–27 MHz range used by CBers around the globe, racking up international contacts.
